# Astrarium: travelling through space and time

**A.R.G.O. — Astrarium Research & Galactic Observatory**
An interactive research book on continuous worldlines, historical skies, and the Phoenix Lights reference encounter.

This Jupyter Book combines the Astrarium whitepaper and model notes with executable equations and parameterized graphs. The calculations are deliberately separated by status: general-relativistic results from cited papers, flat-background kinematic illustrations, and product hypotheses are not interchangeable.

## What the book computes

1. **Two-bubble CTC kinematics.** It evaluates the published symmetric closure condition and a derived flat-background endpoint relation. The endpoint extension does not retarget the paper's full compact-support metric or close a loop through Phoenix.
2. **Oblate single-bubble density slice.** It evaluates an exact integral of Eulerian energy density for a separate prescribed unit-lapse profile. It is not a source calculation for the two-bubble CTC, total energy budget, ADM mass, or engineering design.
3. **Historical context and navigation constraints.** Source-backed celestial data and the Phoenix witness record constrain a hypothetical encounter; they do not supply temporal propulsion or validate a time-travel interpretation.

## Status labels

- **Published geometry:** a mathematical spacetime and worldline result under its stated assumptions.
- **Kinematic illustration:** an equation-level estimate in a simplified frame model.
- **Unsolved extension:** a requirement not yet demonstrated in the cited geometry or current Astrarium implementation.
- **Speculative premise:** a product or narrative hypothesis, such as A.R.G.O. causing a UFO report.

The interactive figures are generated by executing the notebooks at build time. Their source cells remain visible and editable. All input values and generated plots are illustrative unless a cell explicitly identifies a source-backed datum.
