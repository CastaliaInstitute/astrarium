The projector consumes player/app/src/main/assets/sky/ufo-sightings.json as an array of observation records. It does not download or scrape sighting websites. The bundled Colorado pair is synthetic, supplied only to exercise the observation and triangulation display.

Import a redistributable CSV

Use scripts/import_ufo_sightings.py with a CSV containing these required columns:

id,observedAtUtc,latitudeDeg,longitudeDeg

Optional columns are label, timeUncertaintySec, observerAltitudeKm, azimuthDeg, elevationDeg, angularUncertaintyDeg, rangeKm, rangeUncertaintyKm, and eventId. Timestamps must include Z or an explicit UTC offset. Azimuth is clockwise from north in degrees; elevation is degrees above the local horizon. Direction and angular precision must come from the source or a documented measurement process. timeUncertaintySec is required for triangulation and must reflect the recorded timestamp’s actual precision; observations are combined only when their time intervals overlap. observerAltitudeKm is height above the WGS-84 ellipsoid. Set Astrarium’s own site elevation in the dashboard’s sound/location settings to reproject solved positions from the correct horizon origin. When a source omits observer elevation, the solver assumes zero ellipsoid height and labels the derived position accordingly. eventId groups independent observations of one event; do not group reports solely because their dates or descriptions look similar.

Example:

python3 scripts/import_ufo_sightings.py observations.csv \
  player/app/src/main/assets/sky/ufo-sightings.json \
  --source 'Licensed observation archive' \
  --source-url 'https://example.org/dataset' \
  --source-rights 'CC BY 4.0, verified 2026-10-16' \
  --redistribution-permitted --append

The explicit --redistribution-permitted flag is an operator attestation, not a legal review. The importer stores that attestation and the rights reference with every row. Without the flag, it refuses to write. It does not accept NUFORC exports under NUFORC’s current terms, which prohibit scraping and redistributing collections without express written permission. Obtain that permission first if using NUFORC data.

The National Archives UAP metadata guidance defines archival document fields such as record-creation date and city/state location. Those fields help find historical source documents, but they are not necessarily the observation timestamp or a measured sky bearing required to reconstruct a craft position. Astrarium does not turn document metadata into a fabricated sightline.

Source candidate: GEIPAN

GEIPAN’s official CSV announcement describes downloadable case-level and testimony/observation-level CSV files intended for studies. It says witness coordinates are rounded to 0.1 degree (about 10 km in metropolitan France), some fields are omitted or filtered for confidentiality, and complete narratives were still under review. Its technical questionnaire asks for initial/final direction and elevation, and a rough object distance; that does not prove those values are present in the public CSV export for every record. Some individual published cases report more detailed observations; for example, the La Turballe case describes a witness recording GPS coordinates and azimuth. GEIPAN classified that event as a bolide, so it is a useful resolved comparison case, not an unidentified-craft record. Case narratives and attachments are not a substitute for a documented bulk-data schema or reuse license. The CSV download announcement does not state a redistribution license, so verify reuse rights with CNES before packaging the CSV or derived records. Even when a bearing is available, the bulk export’s roughly 10 km observer-location rounding must be carried into any 3D estimate.

Timeline-only source: UAPDrop

UAPDrop’s open dataset states that its compiled records are CC BY 4.0 and include observed time, geocoded coordinates, coordinate precision, and links to source records. Its documented record contract does not list observer azimuth or elevation, so these rows can support a sourced report timeline but cannot locate the observed craft in 3D. Preserve UAPDrop attribution and each originating source_url if using it for that timeline; do not treat its observer coordinates as craft positions.

Archival source requiring case-by-case extraction: Project Blue Book

The U.S. National Archives Project BLUE BOOK collection provides historical case files rather than a normalized sightline dataset. Some scanned reports contain observer locations, observation times, azimuths, and elevations, but completeness and measurement quality vary by case. Treat each extracted observation as a separately sourced record, preserve its NARA catalog reference, and only group reports when the source establishes that they describe the same event. Do not infer a range from a single bearing.

Rows with location and time but no azimuth/elevation are retained as records, but cannot be placed on the sky. A single bearing is rendered only near its observer and does not produce a range estimate. A supplied range can be combined with a bearing to plot a sourced range-based point. Multiple bearings sharing an eventId can produce a least-squares closest point only when their reported time-uncertainty intervals overlap, the directions each have reported angular uncertainty, and their span is no more than ten minutes. Astrarium displays its altitude and ray miss residual as a derived estimate. Missing precision stays missing and prevents triangulation.

The current propagation treats a solved point as stationary during the observation interval. It uses WGS-84 Earth-fixed coordinates and does not estimate a moving trajectory, velocity, object size, or probability distribution. The displayed RMS miss distance is a geometric consistency measure, not a confidence interval. A serious trajectory reconstruction needs synchronized observations, calibrated angular errors, observer elevation, Earth orientation, and a motion model fitted to multiple timed observations.
