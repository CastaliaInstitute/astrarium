Why we built this

Bedtime devices fail in four predictable ways.

We started Atlas after watching kids' projectors break their promises. Ours is built around the four promises most of them can't keep.

They die without Wi-Fi

The bedroom is the worst place in the house to depend on a router. Atlas holds its entire lesson library on-device. Airplane mode, grandma's house, a tent — it just works.

They glow cold blue

Most projectors push bright, cool light at kids at the worst possible hour. Atlas runs warm-amber night optics with a hard brightness floor, plus an audio-only story mode.

They promise 0–10

A 9-month-old and a 9-year-old need opposite pacing. Atlas ships two genuinely separate libraries — 0–3 and 4–7 — built with different tempo, vocabulary, and session length.

They hide the receipt

Lumens, fan noise, optics class, content terms — Atlas publishes all of it before you buy, on this page. If we can't show a spec, we don't sell the device.

They attach a meter to bedtime

Content that costs $9.99/month after month six isn't a product, it's a lease on your routine. Every Atlas lesson ships on the device and stays there. Forever means forever.

They stretch the science

Bedtime routines help kids. Sleep consolidates memory. Those findings are real — but no study shows a specific projector's effect. Our science page keeps those claims separate. Ours should too.

Two libraries, not one mush

Built for an actual age, not a demographic

Every Atlas lesson is banded, paced, and written for one developmental window. The projector knows which library it's in and adjusts session length, transitions, and audio mix accordingly.

0–3

First light

Slow, high-contrast, low-stimulation lessons: faces, shapes, sounds, and lullaby phonics. Sessions capped at 10 minutes with a designed fade-to-dark ending.

The science, scoped honestly

What the research says — and what it doesn't

We're a science-adjacent product, so we owe you precision. Here is each claim behind Atlas, labeled by how much evidence actually supports it.

Consistent bedtime routines support sleep and development

Strong, replicated evidence — Mindell & Williamson 2018; Hale et al. 2011. Atlas is designed as the final step of a routine, not a replacement for one.

Well established

Sleep after learning strengthens children's memory

Strong evidence in children — Axelsson et al. 2016; Peiffer et al. 2020. Lessons before sleep are a reasonable place to reinforce, not a miracle.

Well established

Spaced repetition beats cramming for young children

Well supported — Vlach & Sandhofer 2008. Atlas spaces each concept across a 7-night cycle rather than one long sitting.

Well supported

This specific projector makes kids smarter

No study has measured this device, or any consumer projector, against a control. We won't claim it. Our 30-night trial exists so your family can measure what matters: calmer bedtimes.

Not proven

Our design commitments

Where evidence is thin, we design conservatively instead of marketing loudly.

  • Warm-amber optics with a hard brightness floor — never cool-white at night
  • Audio-only mode for the last third of every session
  • Sessions end in a fade to darkness, never mid-story
  • No autoplay into new content — bedtime isn't a feed
  • Zero data collection from the bedroom; no account needed to run lessons
