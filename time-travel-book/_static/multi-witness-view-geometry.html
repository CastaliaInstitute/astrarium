One witness's apparent track

Gold: the source pulse is arriving at Observer B. Gray: the source lies at this geometric direction, but its photons are not arriving then. Zero angle is the zenith; the sign shows which side of the sky. The plotted track flattens while the witness pauses.

Interpretation: a single physical source does not paint the same sky coordinate for everyone. Nearby witnesses see different elevations and parallax; witnesses outside the beam, below the horizon, or outside the pulse time see nothing. A few kilometres of walking can swing a nearby source across the sky, but have negligible parallax for a source a light-year away (5 km at 1 ly is about 0.0000001 arcsecond). To claim that a pattern resembles a structured craft to several observers, Astrarium needs their worldlines, the light's range and emission history, and each measured angular track. The Star narrative supplies none of those measurements, so these controls explore scenarios rather than recover a unique sighting.
