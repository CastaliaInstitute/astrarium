How much light would an earlier witness see?

Choose a brightness and a source-to-witness distance. The model asks how much visible light a point-like source would need to send into a uniform cone for the witness to receive that flux. It calculates the photon energy at the past event, then divides by a user-selected end-to-end efficiency to estimate the energy drawn from A.R.G.O. The source may be a field-to-light conversion or an explicitly modeled material radiator; the calculator does not create either mechanism.

Field-only conversion: the momentum check

Start with the same spatially uniform modulation, Z(t) = 1 + ε cos(2ωt). It carries pump energy but no net spatial momentum. Photon pairs therefore occupy opposite wavevectors: if one photon travels into the selected witness cone, its partner travels into the opposite cone with equal energy. The readouts estimate the ideal squeezing for this symmetric pair; they expose the extra light that the single-cone budget omits.

For one 550 nm mode pair, Npair = Econe/(hν) = sinh2(sΔt), so the pump supplies at least 2Econe to both cones before losses. With this uniform-pump model, the selected mode receives half the pair energy and the opposite mode receives half. A homogeneous isotropic pump has no preferred direction and distributes pair directions over the sphere: for a 10° cone, only Ω/(4π) = 0.760% of total energy enters the selected cone, with the same fraction in its opposite. The isotropic total-energy readout includes emission in all directions. Higher cone efficiency requires a directional pump or mode selector and its momentum source. A traveling modulation Z(t,x) can in principle steer the pair only if its frequency and wavevector satisfy Ω = ωs + ωi and K = ks + ki. This calculation assumes one frequency, one ideal pair of modes, vacuum input, perfect phase lock, and no loss or pump depletion. That vacuum output has zero mean electric field; a single highly occupied mode is not a steady classical image. The contrast estimate treats the band energy as 550 nm-equivalent photons and assumes independent thermal modes, equal illumination of each image element, and an exposure integrating all modes; it does not design their spatial pattern. A real image needs either a sourced coherent input or a multimode spatial pump plus enough independent modes to average its fluctuations; the pump profile, phase matching, stress-energy, and energy bill are not solved here. A small |ε| is not a small energy bill.

What this calculation does: converts a stated apparent brightness into a conditional photon and energy budget using the AB magnitude convention. AB zero magnitude corresponds to 3631 Jy of spectral flux density (STScI reference). What remains unknown: the Star's true brightness and distance, whether the historical light was a point source, the source spectrum, the photon path through the chronology-changing geometry, and the field-to-light conversion efficiency. A.R.G.O. still needs a continuous channel solution before this optical budget can be called a mission result.
