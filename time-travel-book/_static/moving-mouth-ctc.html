Can a moving wormhole mouth send one pulse into Earth's past?

This comparator integrates a smooth, sub-light-speed mouth history and its proper clock in a flat exterior. The default is an Earth-local target, a round-number 2,000-year sighting age, and a 10 km exit-to-witness range. The date is a scale example, not a proposed identification of the Star. The finite throat, source stress-energy, and Earth world-tube are not solved here.

The solver adds the last ordinary light-travel time to find the mouth-clock lag needed for this sighting age.
The smooth endpoint-motion term is added to this sinusoidal profile; the solver rejects any superluminal samples.

Both mouth clocks keep running forward

The moving clock accumulates less proper time because of its motion. At the final event its reading matches the stationary mouth's earlier reading. This equal-clock map shifts a pulse's exit epoch but preserves the order of a pulse sequence.

What this buys the story: under an assumed traversable-throat map, one continuous signal could be aimed at a past target event while the hull and crew stay home. What it does not buy: a reverse-ordered mind-journey, a way to create or move a wormhole mouth, a solved finite null path, or a safe historical witness intercept. The prescribed moving mouth still travels thousands of light-years; if no matter may make that excursion, this comparator does not satisfy the premise.

Target and sighting are separate events. The exit must be placed on the historical target world-tube. Light then follows a future-directed null path to each witness; its travel time determines the sighting date. This panel uses zero endpoint separation and a 10 km range only as a scale example. Replace them with actual above-surface source and witness worldlines before calling the route a Bethlehem reconstruction.
