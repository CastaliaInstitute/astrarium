Can a moving wormhole mouth send one pulse into Earth's past?

This interactive comparator keeps a smooth, sub-light-speed mouth history and calculates its proper clock. It asks whether a present pulse entering the lagging mouth could match an earlier reading on the stationary mouth. The curve is a flat-exterior kinematic input; the finite throat, source stress-energy, and Earth trajectory are not solved here.

For one present pulse, equal mouth-clock readings pair the moving-mouth entry with this earlier stationary-mouth event.

Both mouth clocks keep running forward

The moving clock accumulates less proper time because it travels at high speed. At return, its reading matches the stationary mouth's earlier reading. The equal-clock relation shifts the pulse's exit epoch but preserves the order of a pulse sequence.

What this buys the story: under the assumed traversable-throat map, one continuous signal can be aimed at a past Earth event while the hull and crew remain at home. What it does not buy: a reverse-ordered mind-journey, a way to create or move a wormhole mouth, or proof the throat has a finite, stable null path. The example also sends the mouth itself on thousands of light-years of travel; if the premise forbids moving any matter, A.R.G.O. needs a different spacetime mechanism.
