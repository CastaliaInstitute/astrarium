A.R.G.O. as the Star of Bethlehem#

A.R.G.O. — Astrarium Research & Galactic Observatory The lead mission is A.R.G.O.’s journey back through space and time to become the Star of Bethlehem. The interactive chapters compute its worldline estimates and compare the sky A.R.G.O. creates with natural celestial reconstructions.

This Jupyter Book combines the Astrarium whitepaper and model notes with executable equations and parameterized graphs. The calculations are deliberately separated by status: general-relativistic results from cited papers, flat-background kinematic illustrations, and mission premises, published results, and engineering claims are not interchangeable.

Start here#

  • Read the whitepaper for mission goals, data, hazards, and the implementation plan.

  • Review the temporal-drive candidate for the two-bubble CTC, wormhole comparator, single-bubble limitations, and the unresolved Bethlehem encounter constraint.

  • Open the two-bubble calculator · download its Jupyter notebook to explore the CTC speed threshold and the large interstellar scale implied by the published symmetric example.

  • Open the saucer-profile calculator · download its Jupyter notebook to calculate the Eulerian density slice for one explicit oblate unit-lapse ansatz.

You can also run either notebook in Google Colab: two-bubble kinematics · saucer profile.

What the book computes#

  1. Two-bubble CTC kinematics. It evaluates the published symmetric closure condition and a derived flat-background endpoint relation. The long-lookback example is a scale illustration; the endpoint extension does not retarget the paper’s full compact-support metric or close a loop through Bethlehem.

  2. Oblate single-bubble density slice. It evaluates an exact integral of Eulerian energy density for a separate prescribed unit-lapse profile. It is not a source calculation for the two-bubble CTC, total energy budget, ADM mass, or engineering design.

  3. Historical context and navigation constraints. The Star of Bethlehem story is treated as an underconstrained observation narrative. A.R.G.O. is the source in the mission model; natural-sky reconstructions, including Jupiter, provide comparison views.

Status labels#

  • Published geometry: a mathematical spacetime and worldline result under its stated assumptions.

  • Kinematic illustration: an equation-level estimate in a simplified frame model.

  • Unsolved extension: a requirement not yet demonstrated in the cited geometry or current Astrarium implementation.

  • Mission premise: A.R.G.O. is the Star described in Matthew 2.

In this book, A.R.G.O. is the Star described in Matthew 2, and the mission follows its continuous journey through spacetime. The interactive figures are generated by executing the notebooks at build time. Their source cells remain visible and editable. All input values and generated plots are illustrative unless a cell explicitly identifies a source-backed datum.
