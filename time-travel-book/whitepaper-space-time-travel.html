A poetic physics of Zoom#

To zoom is to choose a scale. To look across the universe is also to choose an age. The Moon we see is already a little over a second past; sunlight arrives with an eight-minute-old story; a galaxy millions of light-years away appears as it was millions of years ago. Distance lays out history like pages in a book, and every photon is a messenger that began its journey before our eyes were ready to receive it.

A.R.G.O. adds a third meaning to the same small word. In the lead mind-journey, the crew remains in the present while the chronal channel lets attention follow a continuous sequence of remote events and writes a visible pattern into the selected historical scene. In the separate hull-transit comparison, the saucer itself enters a time-shifted throat. In either model, a quantum-entangled register can act as a story’s cosmic index: correlated measurements may help label a scene, but do not transport the ship or the scene. The chronal field carries view-data and makes the optical appearance in the lead mission; the throat carries the hull only in the comparison model.

This is poetic physics with an engineering ledger. Ordinary telescopes already let us look backward because light takes time to travel. Entanglement already gives nature correlations with no classical counterpart, but it cannot by itself send a chosen message. A.R.G.O.’s channel is the science-fiction law we add between those facts. We can give that law precise equations and test the consequences inside the book’s model, while keeping clear which parts belong to the measured universe and which belong to the imagined instrument.

The mind-journey has three ledgers. Geometry records the information path and where each event lies. Information records what scene is encoded, how quickly it is sampled, and how uncertainty grows. Experience records how the local crew turns that stream into a moving point of view while their bodies remain at home. Hull-transit mode adds the material vehicle’s worldline, finite envelope, and mouth histories. Quantum entanglement can give either instrument a shared reference and help compare patterns; it does not carry the ship or the scene. None of these ledgers can be replaced by a glowing line on a map.

The telescope’s ordinary look-back#

Zoom is time travel for the eyes. We stay here; the light brings us a view from then. The telescope is our quiet time machine: no one boards it, and no one leaves Earth. We point, gather the arriving light, and read the sky as it looked when that light began its journey.

Astronomy already gives us a friendly kind of “Zoom through time.” A telescope makes a distant object easier to see, but it does not carry us toward it. It collects light that is arriving here now. Because light takes time to travel, the view shows the object as it was when that light began its journey: about 1.3 seconds ago for the Moon, 8 minutes 20 seconds ago for the Sun, and roughly 2.5 million years ago for Andromeda. We look back without leaving Earth. NASA explains this as looking back in time (Webb; Hubble).

Here is the lovely twist—and a useful distinction. Turning up a telescope’s magnification does not make the light older. If we keep looking at the same galaxy, its photons have the same journey time whether we see a small image or a close-up. We look farther back when we choose a more distant source: its light has crossed more space before meeting us. A deep-field image feels like a Zoom into the past because greater distance often means greater look-back time, not because the lens turns a time dial. The galaxy is not travelling into our room. Its old light is. Zoom is time travel in what we can see, not in where our bodies go. We receive a message from the past; we do not send one back.

Old light arrives at one stationary observer. Horizontal lengths are not to scale.

Magnification is not look-back time#

There are two different sliders hidden inside the word “Zoom.” Magnification changes how large a source looks across the sky. Distance or redshift changes which earlier emission event supplied the light arriving now. For a nearby object in a nearly static region, the look-back time is approximately \(t_L=D/c\): one light-year of distance corresponds to about one year of photon travel. That shortcut is not accurate across the expanding universe, where the space between galaxies changes while the light is travelling.

For a distant galaxy, astronomers use its cosmological redshift \(z\) and a model of expansion. In a spatially flat matter-plus-radiation-plus-dark-energy model,

\[ H(z)=H_0\sqrt{\Omega_r(1+z)^4+\Omega_m(1+z)^3+\Omega_\Lambda},\qquad t_L(z)=\int_0^z\frac{dz'}{(1+z')H(z')},\qquad D_C(z)=c\int_0^z\frac{dz'}{H(z')}. \]

Here \(t_L\) is the light’s look-back time, while \(D_C\) is the present-day comoving distance; they are different quantities. The factor \(1+z\) also tells how much the universe stretched the light’s wavelength on its way here. The interactive uses a flat-\(\Lambda\)CDM example with \(H_0=67.4\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}\), \(\Omega_m=0.315\), and a small radiation term; Planck’s measurements motivate the first two values, and changing the cosmological model changes the conversion (Planck Collaboration, 2018). At \(z=1\), this model gives about \(7.95\) billion years of look-back and \(11.09\) billion light-years of present-day comoving distance; the received wavelength is twice its emitted wavelength. This is a computed reconstruction of when received light left—not a route followed by the telescope, observer, or galaxy.

This is the real inspiration for the mind-journey: the crew can look far away while their bodies stay home. The mission asks for a much bigger connection—to experience a selected event in Earth’s past and perhaps make a light visible to a witness there. A telescope’s ordinary look-back only receives light that can already reach us; it cannot send information to an earlier event. The proposed A.R.G.O. channel is a separate, unsolved part of the mission.

A small poem with a large light cone#

The sky is not a picture pinned to one moment. It is a stack of messages arriving at once: moonlight a heartbeat old, sunlight eight minutes old, starlight perhaps centuries old, and galaxy light older still. Every telescope points through space, but every telescope also points along a path through time. We call the shape of all the places whose light can reach us our past light cone. It is not a tunnel we travel down; it is the set of messages that can meet us here.

So “Zoom” has three viewing meanings in this book. Optical zoom enlarges the same picture. Astronomical Zoom points us toward a more distant source, whose arriving light can show an earlier cosmic scene. A.R.G.O.’s mind’s-eye Zoom is the science-fiction extension: choose an event-address and let a continuous chronal echo compose a viewpoint from information ordinary light has not delivered here. The physical hull-transit mission is a separate fourth operation: A.R.G.O. itself follows a timelike path through a time-shifted throat. The mind’s-eye mode keeps the ship home; the hull mode does not. Each needs its own route and physics.

Here is the more adventurous quantum metaphor. Entanglement is a shared score: distant instruments can find correlations that ordinary matching coins cannot reproduce. The score might help fictional A.R.G.O. label a pattern; it does not carry the melody, choose the tune, or send a message. In the story, the chronal field is the invented courier, and the dome is where the crew hears it as a moving sky. Turn off that courier and the shared score remains beautiful—but no past scene arrives on demand. That separation lets us be bold with the fiction and exact about which part is new.

For a child: the universe is a storybook whose pages are events. Starlight lets us read pages whose glowing letters are already on their way to us. Entanglement gives two readers matching bookmarks. A.R.G.O.’s imagined echo is the new rule that lets one reader follow a chosen page continuously. The bookmarks help identify the page; they do not turn into a telephone. That last step belongs to our fiction, and our equations must show what the telephone costs.

Temporal magnification: how fast does the page turn?#

We can make the imagined “Zoom” precise by giving each remote scene a time address, \(q\), and tracking which address the crew experiences at each onboard proper time, \(\tau_A\). For a steady sweep through the past, use the simple map

\[ q(\tau_A)=q_0-\alpha\tau_A,\qquad \alpha=\frac{T_{\rm span}}{\Delta\tau_A}. \]

\(q_0\) is the remote epoch at the start of the scan (often “now”), \(T_{\rm span}\) is how far back the scan reaches, and \(\Delta\tau_A\) is the time that passes on A.R.G.O. The minus sign means the chosen address moves toward earlier events as the crew’s clock moves forward. The magnitude \(|dq/d\tau_A|=\alpha\) is the temporal magnification: how much remote time the selected view sweeps through per second aboard. It is a clock-map rate, not a velocity through space and not a signal speed.

For example, mapping 5,000 years of history into five onboard minutes gives \(\alpha\approx5.26\times10^8\): each second aboard corresponds to about 16.7 years of remote history. At 24 displayed frames per second, neighboring frames would be about 0.69 remote years apart. A continuous address map therefore does not automatically give a smooth, detailed movie. The channel must carry enough changing information to resolve the scene, and the display must preserve the details the crew is meant to notice. A slow-changing star pattern may survive a coarse scan; a person waving or a brief flash may not. The required information rate depends on the scene’s fastest relevant changes and the chosen image resolution.

This is where the poetic idea becomes a useful engineering question. Ordinary astronomical Zoom chooses photons whose emission dates are already encoded by their travel time. A.R.G.O.’s fictional Zoom chooses \(q(\tau_A)\) directly, then asks a new channel to supply the information for those events. Quantum entanglement might act as a shared event label in the story, but standard quantum mechanics does not let the crew choose a distant measurement outcome or send a controllable picture through entanglement alone. The added chronal interaction must carry the scene data, obey its own bandwidth and noise limits, and explain how a present choice can be correlated with an earlier event without producing contradictions.

In the simplest story model, we can dial \(\alpha\) and draw the requested sweep. In a physical model, \(\alpha\) must emerge from the field equations and boundary conditions; it cannot be typed into a slider and called a drive. A continuous \(q(\tau_A)\) is the itinerary for the mind’s view. The solved signal or field path that makes that view possible is a separate object.

In short: we are building a navigation plan for a mission that treats time travel as possible. The book is free to imagine new physics; it earns that freedom by stating the new rule, calculating its consequences, and keeping it distinct from results already established in physics.

1. Physical status and the continuity requirement#

This proposal separates three claims that are easy to conflate:

  1. Ordinary relativistic time dilation is established physics. A traveler can accumulate less proper time than observers on a chosen reference worldline and still move toward their future. This does not provide a route into an earlier Earth epoch.

  2. General relativity admits mathematical spacetimes with closed timelike curves (CTCs), subject to global and physical caveats. A CTC is a continuous timelike worldline that returns to an event in its own causal past. A signal that reaches a past event also needs causal structure that allows the relevant pastward connection. Such solutions are not evidence that a usable channel can be built. Proposed constructions can require unusual stress-energy or assumptions whose physical realizability is unsettled. Hawking’s chronology-protection conjecture argues that quantum effects may prevent macroscopic chronology violation; it is a conjecture, not a proved universal theorem.

  3. A.R.G.O.’s hull-transit and mind-journey are different mission hypotheses. The software may explore each conditionally, but must not describe either as a consequence of ephemerides. Hull transit requires a continuous timelike worldline in a specified metric; mind-journey requires a separately specified information path and view map. Neither is supplied by a slider or by demonstrated neurophysics.

A.R.G.O.’s quantum “focus” mechanism#

Here is a more precise science-fiction role for quantum theory. Entanglement supplies a shared correlation reference between A.R.G.O.’s resonators; calibrated, phase-locked clocks provide the timing and phase reference. A two-boundary measurement, or postselection, acts like a focus: it asks which intermediate pattern is consistent with both the prepared state and a later selected detector outcome. A new chronal field then carries the focused pattern along a continuous path. The three jobs are deliberately different: entanglement registers correlations, postselection filters histories, and the chronal field transports the usable record.

The filter has a standard quantum-mechanics toy model. Prepare a qubit in \(|+x\rangle=(|0\rangle+|1\rangle)/\sqrt2\), measure \(Z\) at an intermediate event, and keep only runs whose later detector finds \(|0\rangle\). The Aharonov–Bergmann–Lebowitz rule gives

\[ P(z_j\mid i,f)=\frac{|\langle f|P_j|i\rangle|^2}{\sum_k|\langle f|P_k|i\rangle|^2}, \qquad P_0=|0\rangle\langle0|,\quad P_1=|1\rangle\langle1|. \]

For \(|i\rangle=|+x\rangle\) and \(|f\rangle=|0\rangle\), the selected sample has \(P(z_0\mid i,f)=1\) and \(P(z_1\mid i,f)=0\). But only half the trials pass the final filter: \(P(f\mid i)=|\langle0|+x\rangle|^2=1/2\). Before the final outcome is known, the intermediate results are still 50/50. Postselection can make a conditioned pattern look sharply chosen; it does not let someone send a chosen bit backward in time. The accepted-run flag is itself information and normally has to be reported over an ordinary channel.

In A.R.G.O.’s fictional instrument, the chronal field is the new ingredient that carries that accepted-run record to the crew while the chosen scene address \(q(\tau_A)\) moves continuously toward earlier events. It must also pay for rejected runs, finite bandwidth, decoherence, and the energy in any visible photons produced at the historical endpoint. The field’s Green function must come from one specified geometry and have a finite, self-consistent solution; the postselection equation does not create that geometry. This makes the speculative leap crisp and testable inside the story: does the chronal field deliver the acceptance record and scene data, or does A.R.G.O. merely display a pattern selected after ordinary-time comparison?

Minimum realizable chronal channel#

The effective kernel above is useful for requirements work, but it is not yet a dynamical model. To make the gap visible, represent one resolved scene component by an input amplitude \(a_X(q)\) at the remote event and an output amplitude \(a_A(\tau)\) in the dome. Normalize both amplitudes so their squared magnitudes represent power in their respective local frames. A linear, time-varying channel has the form

\[ a_A(\tau)=\int dq\;G(\tau,q)\,a_X(q)+n_A(\tau),\qquad \langle n_A(\tau)n_A^*(\tau')\rangle=N_A(\tau,\tau'). \]

\(G\) is the Green response of the proposed physical field, including its source and detector couplings and boundary conditions appropriate to the proposed geometry; \(n_A\) is irreducible channel and detector noise. The scene-selection map \(q(\tau)\) is a requested sampling schedule. It is not a substitute for \(G\). A working model must derive both from a field equation, metric, boundary conditions, and initial state. If it simply inserts a narrow peak \(G\propto g_{\sigma_q}(q-q(\tau))\), it has specified what the desired device does, not how the device does it.

The inverse problem is then concrete: can one reconstruct a stated scene component from \(a_A\) with bounded error? For a band-limited classical signal, a necessary capacity condition is \(R_{\rm info}\leq B\log_2(1+S/N)\), where \(B\) is the channel bandwidth and \(S/N\) is the received signal-to-noise ratio over that band. The information-rate demand \(R_{\rm info}\) must be calculated from the chosen scene encoding; a recognizable sequence of sparse markers may need far less than a full video, but the encoding cannot be left implicit. A candidate fails if the requested rate exceeds capacity, if the transfer function has no response at a required scene mode, or if the output is indistinguishable from noise at the stated confidence.

Energy bookkeeping is equally direct. For a passive channel, the integrated output energy cannot exceed the coupled input energy after losses. If the chronal field supplies gain, its stress-energy must account for output energy, recoil, entropy production, and metric backreaction. For a time-invariant linear response using the convention \(e^{-i\omega t}\), poles of \(G(\omega)\) must lie below the real-frequency axis for a decaying stable response; a real-axis pole is marginal, and an upper-half-plane pole grows in time. A growing mode requires saturation or a different nonlinear solution, not a finite steady-state prediction from the linear model. These are necessary tests, not a sufficient proof of a chronology-violating solution. The model still has to exhibit a continuous path and show that its endpoint events are causally connected in its declared geometry.

Until that calculation exists, the kernel and minute-long remote scan are useful design targets, but the current book has not computed a physical chronal signal or established that the mission can be realized.

Quantum links as coordinates, not spacecraft#

We can push the quantum idea further without asking entanglement to do a job it cannot do. Imagine a network of quantum sensors distributed through the fictional bulk, each sharing entangled pairs with A.R.G.O. Their correlations could provide matching coordinate marks: when local records are eventually compared, they could help identify which sensors sampled related field modes and reconstruct the network geometry. For a bipartite state \(\rho_{AB}\), the quantum mutual information is

\[ I(A{:}B)=S(\rho_A)+S(\rho_B)-S(\rho_{AB}),\qquad S(\rho)=-\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\log_2\rho). \]

It measures correlations, not a message sent from \(A\) to \(B\). A Bell pair has \(I(A{:}B)=2\) bits, but neither side can choose its random measurement result to encode a word. The pair is like two beautifully correlated dice, not a faster-than-light telephone. Quantum teleportation does not change this: the receiver needs classical bits before the state can be reconstructed (Bennett et al., 1993).

The science-fiction engineering leap is to use changes in the pattern of correlations as a probe of hidden geometry. Let \(C_{ij}=\langle\sigma_i\otimes\sigma_j\rangle\) be the measured correlation between sensors \(i\) and \(j\). A chronal medium might have a response kernel \(K(q,\tau)\) between A.R.G.O.’s clock reading \(\tau\) and remote-event label \(q\). The network could then be modeled by

\[ C_{ij}(\tau)=\int dq\;K(q,\tau)\,G_{ij}(q)+\epsilon_{ij}(\tau), \]

where \(G_{ij}(q)\) is the correlation pattern predicted for the network at event \(q\), and \(\epsilon_{ij}\) collects detector noise and model error. Here \(q\) is a dimensionless event coordinate, and the kernel is normalized so \(\int K(q,\tau)\,dq=\eta\), with \(0\leq\eta\leq1\) the response efficiency. This is an inverse problem: infer the kernel from measurements. Mutual information alone is not a distance meter; a geometry reconstruction needs an independently specified rule connecting field correlations to spacetime geometry. In standard physics, this equation is a data-analysis model only when the records can be compared through ordinary causal channels. The fictional extension says that the chronal medium makes a selected remote pattern locally available in minutes. It must provide its own dynamics, bandwidth, noise floor, energy source, and backreaction; no known entanglement protocol supplies them.

Why might this feel like a journey of mind? Imagine the dome receives reconstructed field patterns one after another. A neural or computer display turns them into a moving viewpoint, just as a planetarium turns many measurements into a coherent sky. The crew experiences motion through a scene while the hull remains still. Continuity belongs to the sequence of decoded views; coordinates belong to the inferred geometry; the ship’s worldline remains local. This is a useful science-fiction design, but it does not make the crew, consciousness, or information physically travel unless the new kernel is a propagating channel in the story’s field equations.

The test is concrete. With the chronal coupling off, local measurements must obey ordinary no-signalling: changing a distant measurement setting cannot change A.R.G.O.’s reduced density matrix \(\rho_A\). With it on, the model predicts a selectable shift \(\delta p(a\mid x)\) in local outcome probabilities before ordinary records arrive. Its size must exceed stated noise and detector drift, with a specified dependence on distance, event-label separation, and coupling strength. If there is no such local signature, the entangled network can still help reconstruct a map afterward—but it has not become the engine of a minutes-long journey through light-years.

A deeper quantum analogy: time told by a pair of clocks#

There is another, subtler use for entanglement. In the Page–Wootters relational-time construction, a clock and another system can be entangled in a joint state that is stationary overall. An observer who asks, “What is the system doing when the clock reads \(t\)?” can recover an evolving conditional state. In a schematic idealization,

\[ |\Omega\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathcal N}}\int dt\,|t\rangle_C\otimes e^{-i\hat H_S t/\hbar}|\psi_0\rangle_S, \qquad |\psi(t)\rangle_S\propto{}_C\!\langle t|\Omega\rangle. \]

In the ideal construction the global state obeys a stationary constraint \((\hat H_C+\hat H_S)|\Omega\rangle=0\), with the clock Hamiltonian convention chosen to generate translations of \(|t\rangle_C\). The integral suppresses the finite resolution and normalization details of a physical clock. The clock reading is a relational label: it tells us which system-state is correlated with that clock-state. The joint state does not need to “flow” in an extra outside time for the conditional story to have a sequence. This is a genuine quantum-theory idea, developed as a way to describe dynamics using correlations (Page and Wootters, 1983; see also Marletto and Vedral, 2017). It does not let an observer choose a distant clock reading and receive its state instantly, does not move information outside a light cone, and does not make a past event observable at A.R.G.O. on demand.

For the story, imagine a local clock register \(C_A\) entangled with an event register \(C_X\). Ordinary relational quantum mechanics might let their correlations define a shared sequence after the two sets of records can be compared. The fictional chronal lens adds a new conditional-access rule: when the crew’s clock reads \(\tau_A\), the receiver can condition on an event-register value \(q\) according to a narrow kernel \(K(q,\tau_A)\). The displayed scene is then a conditional state,

\[ \rho_{\rm view}(\tau_A)=\frac{\int dq\,K(q,\tau_A)\,\rho_X(q)}{\int dq\,K(q,\tau_A)}, \qquad q(\tau_A)=q_0+R(\tau_A-\tau_0), \]

where \(\rho_X(q)\) is the physical state of the remote scene at event-label \(q\), and \(R\) sets how quickly the imagined viewpoint scans the event sequence. For this mixture interpretation, take \(K\ge0\) and require its integral to be finite and nonzero; a genuinely coherent transfer would instead need an amplitude-level map and its own quantum-channel constraints. This is a useful way to describe the experience: the crew’s local proper time advances, while the conditional view is indexed by a remote history. But the equation is a receiver rule, not yet a mechanism. To become a real channel in the fiction, \(K\) must be derived from a local field and geometry, obey a stated energy-and-momentum ledger, have finite bandwidth and noise, and specify how the remote scene couples to the field. If \(K\) gives A.R.G.O. controllable access to a remote state before any causal signal can arrive, the theory intentionally departs from ordinary no-signalling quantum mechanics and must say exactly how.

This suggests a useful poetic line for the book: entanglement can be the grammar of the clocks; the chronal lens is the invented verb that lets the mind read another page. The grammar can make a coherent sequence out of correlations. Only the fictional verb supplies the impossible reach. The crew does not ride a photon, and the equations do not claim consciousness is a quantum signal; the scene is reconstructed experience whose physical input still needs a new, testable channel. The view remains continuous when \(K\) changes smoothly with \(\tau_A\) and neighboring clock readings select overlapping event ranges. A discontinuous change of event label would be a cut in the film, not the continuous journey the mission requires.

One more possibility is that the network transmits no scene at all. It may instead change which histories are compatible at a boundary, rather as post-selection changes conditional probabilities in carefully selected laboratory data. A.R.G.O. could act as a “history interferometer,” comparing candidate event histories and registering those that match a chosen boundary condition. This is mathematically suggestive, but post-selection alone cannot choose a rare outcome on demand or create a closed timelike curve. The story must add a controllable boundary-condition mechanism and account for its success probability, entropy, and causal consistency. Here the chronal echo is the proposed channel; entangled sensors and boundary selection are ways the fictional instrument addresses and decodes it.

A two-boundary quantum telescope: letting the future help frame the past#

There is a stranger way to use quantum theory in the story. Ordinary prediction starts with a prepared state and asks what may happen next. Quantum experiments can also ask a conditional question: if a system was prepared in one state and later found in another, what can we infer about an intervening measurement? This is called pre- and post-selection. It makes the future measurement part of the description of the past without sending a message backward in time. The Aharonov–Bergmann–Lebowitz rule gives the conditional probability for an intermediate outcome \(a\):

\[ P(a\mid\psi_i,\psi_f)=\frac{|\langle\psi_f|\Pi_a|\psi_i\rangle|^2}{\sum_b|\langle\psi_f|\Pi_b|\psi_i\rangle|^2}, \]

where \(|\psi_i\rangle\) is the prepared state, \(\langle\psi_f|\) describes the later selected result, and \(\Pi_a\) projects onto the outcome being tested. This is a rule for updating probabilities when both ends of an experiment are specified. It does not let an experimenter choose the later result at will, and it does not make the earlier detector’s statistics carry a controllable message. The original time-symmetric measurement paper develops this conditional framework.

For A.R.G.O., imagine an event-address register entangled with a receiver inside the dome. The register has basis states \(|q\rangle\), each tagged to a candidate event in the sky’s history. A conventional telescope estimates \(q\) from photons that have already arrived. The fictional telescope instead asks whether a boundary condition at A.R.G.O. can select one event-address from a continuous family, while an echo field supplies the missing physical connection. The entanglement is the matching key: it helps the receiver recognize which pattern belongs with which event. The field is still the courier.

Here is the sharp science-fiction hinge. In standard quantum mechanics, a later boundary condition can change a conditional description of earlier results, but it cannot be turned into a knob that changes earlier local statistics. The story adds a new chronal susceptibility \(\chi(q,\tau)\): a coupling between the receiver’s local clock reading \(\tau\) and a remote event label \(q\). A simple fictional response kernel is

\[ K_R(q,\tau)=\frac{\eta}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\sigma_q}\exp\!\left[-\frac{(q-q_0+R(\tau-\tau_0))^2}{2\sigma_q^2}\right],\qquad \int K_R(q,\tau)\,dq=\eta, \]

so its peak follows \(q(\tau)=q_0-R(\tau-\tau_0)\). The parameter \(R>0\) is the rate at which the viewed event-label runs toward earlier values; \(\sigma_q\) is the blur in that label; and \(\eta\) is the surviving response amplitude. The kernel is smooth, so the selected viewpoint has no date-jumps. A possible microscopic story-law would couple event operators through a nonlocal interaction,

\[ S_{\rm chronal}=\hbar g\int d\tau\,dq\;K_R(q,\tau)\,\hat O_A(\tau)\hat O_X(q), \]

with dimensionless operators \(\hat O_A,\hat O_X\) and \(g\) carrying the units needed to make the action dimensionless. This equation is not derived from ordinary quantum field theory: \(\hat O_X(q)\) at an earlier event cannot influence A.R.G.O. through a standard retarded field in a globally hyperbolic spacetime. The coupling is the invented law that the rest of the fictional theory must earn.

The idea is therefore a two-boundary quantum telescope, not “entanglement sends the crew through time.” One boundary helps choose the question; the other records a continuous local experience. A hypothetical chronal susceptibility joins them, and the echo field carries the answer. Turn off \(g\) or \(\eta\), and A.R.G.O. returns to ordinary quantum statistics: it can look at old light already on its way here, but it cannot query an event outside its causal past light cone. Turn the new law on, and its first testable consequence is a selected change in local outcomes before any ordinary signal could arrive. That would violate standard no-signalling and must come with a numerical signal strength, background noise, energy cost, backreaction, and an account of causal loops. In the story, that violation is the instrument’s engine; in real physics, it is the line the engine has crossed.

A child-sized picture: the universe is a book whose pages are events. Entanglement gives two readers matching bookmarks. A future measurement can help us infer which sentence was written earlier, but it cannot whisper the sentence across the room. A.R.G.O.’s fictional chronal field is the impossible whisper—made continuous, measurable, and costly enough that the equations have to keep track of every word.

A quantum consistency filter: post-selection#

There is a more technical quantum idea for deciding which self-referential histories survive. In post-selected teleportation, prepare two systems in a maximally entangled state, let one interact with the payload, then keep only trials in which a later measurement finds a chosen entangled outcome. The retained data behave mathematically like a quantum channel conditioned on a future boundary condition. Researchers have used this construction to model closed-timelike-curve behavior and to test its predictions with ordinary laboratory circuits; those experiments simulate the model through selection of measurement records. They do not create a spacetime loop or send a message to yesterday (Lloyd et al., theory; Lloyd et al., optical experiment). This is a competing quantum consistency prescription, not an extra layer on top of the Deutsch rule adopted below for the reference model.

For a \(d\)-level payload, a particular maximally entangled Bell outcome in ordinary teleportation occurs with probability \(1/d^2\) when the interaction is the identity. If the payload is \(n\) independent qubits, \(d=2^n\), so the chosen outcome occurs with probability

\[ p_{\rm herald}=d^{-2}=4^{-n},\qquad \log_{10}p_{\rm herald}=-n\log_{10}4\simeq-0.60206n. \]

The selected state can be written as a normalized conditional map,

\[ \rho\longmapsto\frac{C\rho C^\dagger}{\operatorname{Tr}(C\rho C^\dagger)},\qquad C=\operatorname{Tr}_{\rm loop}U, \]

where \(U\) describes the interaction with the loop register. The normalization depends on the input state, which is why the conditional rule is nonlinear. In a real laboratory the rejected trials still exist; one cannot force the desired result to occur on demand. The fictional chronal controller would have to promote this conditional rule into a global law of nature and explain how its success condition is enforced without a controllable post-selection device.

This gives A.R.G.O. a measurable design tension. If we naïvely encoded the book’s uncompressed 1080p color stream as one qubit per bit, \(n\simeq9.42\times10^{19}\) and the specific-outcome probability would be about \(10^{-5.67\times10^{19}}\). That number is not a universal limit on every imaginable quantum channel: a compressed scene, a different interaction, or a different consistency law changes the model. It does show why “entanglement sends the whole picture” is not a useful engineering specification. A.R.G.O. needs a stated scene code, a success rule, an error rate, and a bandwidth budget; if it relies on rare post-selection, its claimed minutes-long excursion must account for the probability that the chronal query simply fails.

Post-selection can therefore serve as a quantum consistency filter in the story, while the chronal echo remains the signal path. A quantum circuit or a boundary condition does not by itself draw a continuous route through spacetime. To honor the mission’s no-jump rule, every history that survives the filter must still correspond to one connected field solution in the chosen geometry, with continuous events, wave-vector, and stress-energy at each join. The P-CTC prescription is also one choice among inequivalent quantum consistency rules; critics have identified paradoxes in its broader use, so the book must treat it as a candidate rule rather than a settled account of nature (Ralph’s critique).

A candidate signal law: the advanced massive-field tail#

Here is a second, more sharply defined piece of speculative physics. Give A.R.G.O. a neutral scalar field \(\Psi\) obeying a Klein–Gordon equation,

\[ \left(\Box_g-\frac{m_\Psi^2c^2}{\hbar^2}\right)\Psi(x)=J(x). \]

The equation itself is familiar. Its Green function is not unique until we choose boundary conditions. The retarded Green function lets a source influence its future; the advanced Green function has support in the source’s past. Both solve the same local wave equation. Standard physical preparation selects retarded behavior for controllable signals. A.R.G.O.’s science-fiction postulate is a future-boundary transducer that can prepare and control an advanced solution:

\[ \Psi_{\rm adv}(x)=\int G_{\rm adv}(x,x')J_A(x')\,dV',\qquad \operatorname{supp}G_{\rm adv}(\cdot,x')\subseteq J^-(x'). \]

Here \(J_A\) must be a finite, smooth source distributed across A.R.G.O.’s world-tube; an ideal point impulse is useful for deriving the Green function, but its singular field is not a realizable transmitter.

This does not make a disturbance outrun light. The field can only connect events inside the causal cone. For a massless field in flat 3+1-dimensional space, the Green function lies on the light cone; for a massive Klein–Gordon field it also has a tail inside the cone. A.R.G.O. and an ancient event on nearly the same terrestrial worldline are timelike separated, so a massive tail is the relevant mathematical feature. In the ordinary retarded problem it carries information from the ancient event toward the later ship. The fiction changes the boundary condition so the later ship participates in selecting the earlier field. That is a real reversal of cause and effect, even though the field equation and its continuous local solutions are familiar. The massive-scalar tail is a standard result; the controllable advanced transducer is not (Yaremko).

Causal-path audit. An advanced Green function is a boundary-value rule, not a future-directed worldline running from A.R.G.O. to the ancient event. In a globally hyperbolic spacetime, no continuous future-directed causal curve connects a later event \(S\) to an earlier event \(X\). A smooth field value at both events does not supply that missing curve. To meet the mission’s no-jump navigation requirement, either embed the field in an explicit chronology-violating geometry and integrate its continuous path through that geometry, or keep the advanced solution labeled as a nonlocal causal postulate rather than a traveled route. If A.R.G.O.’s present setting is supposed to choose the past flash, solve the complete future-boundary problem—including the field’s return, the light seen by witnesses, and a self-consistency rule—before claiming controllable signaling. This is the decisive difference between a mathematically allowed advanced solution and an operational time-drive.

The mass scale is not arbitrary if the field is meant to remain coherent across a long timelike separation. The tail’s phase depends on the dimensionless quantity \(m_\Psi c^2\Delta\tau/\hbar\). Setting that quantity to order one for \(\Delta\tau=2{,}000\) Julian years gives

\[ m_\Psi\sim\frac{\hbar}{c^2\Delta\tau}=1.86\times10^{-62}\,\mathrm{kg},\qquad \frac{m_\Psi c^2}{1\,\mathrm{eV}}\sim1.04\times10^{-26},\qquad \bar\lambda_\Psi=\frac{\hbar}{m_\Psi c}\sim2{,}000\,\mathrm{ly}. \]

This is a characteristic phase scale, not a derived particle mass, a measured dark-matter candidate, or proof that a usable signal survives. It says that a field whose Compton clock ticks much faster than the two-millennia separation will accumulate a rapidly varying phase across the historical link. Its tail strength, bandwidth, noise, coupling to matter, and stability still have to be calculated.

To couple the field to light, one candidate is the gauge-invariant interaction \(\mathcal L_{\rm int}=-(g_{\gamma\Psi}/4)\Psi F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}\) in natural units. It changes the electromagnetic kinetic term and must be varied together with the scalar and source action; the derived equations are given below. A static \(\Psi\) with no charged current leaves \(F_{\mu\nu}=0\) as a solution, so this coupling is not by itself a light bulb. Photon production needs a physical time-dependent pump or charged emitter, and the field, resonator, emitter, and photons must exchange a conserved total stress-energy. A visible pattern also requires finite source size, directionality, and spectrum; the coupling alone specifies none of those.

The energy scale makes this separation essential. For the 2,000-year phase choice above, one scalar quantum at rest carries \(m_\Psi c^2\simeq1.67\times10^{-45}\,\mathrm J=1.04\times10^{-26}\,\mathrm{eV}\), corresponding to \(m_\Psi c^2/h\simeq2.52\times10^{-12}\,\mathrm{Hz}\). A 550 nm photon carries about \(2.25\,\mathrm{eV}\) at \(5.45\times10^{14}\,\mathrm{Hz}\)—roughly \(2.2\times10^{26}\) times more energy per quantum. This ultralight field can be a slowly varying control or phase field; it cannot be treated as a one-quantum visible-light source. A conversion model would need to tap an enormous coherent field population or, more plausibly, trigger a separate powered emitter at the past event, then show where that emitter’s energy comes from. For scale, the point-source scenario in Appendix B uses 22 mJ of visible output and 2.2 J at A.R.G.O. under a 1% assumed channel efficiency. Supplying 22 mJ from these scalar quanta alone would require at least \(1.3\times10^{43}\) quanta even at perfect energy conversion; real coupling losses raise the requirement. This count is an energy bookkeeping bound, not evidence that such a coherent state or coupling can be prepared.

There is a useful hard limit. A fixed advanced link between two worldlines gives a time offset, approximately \(q=\tau_A-\Delta\), and therefore \(dq/d\tau_A=+1\). It can place a single field pulse in an earlier era, but it does not make later control pulses touch progressively earlier scenes. The required reverse-playback map \(dq/d\tau_A<0\) still needs an orientation-reversing boundary operator or a different global geometry. This candidate therefore offers a testable field-equation template for one pastward interaction, not a solved manifestation or the entire mind-journey. Its key new rule is controllability of an advanced boundary condition; it must not be hidden inside the word “entanglement.”

Route choice for the rest of this chapter. The advanced massive-field solution above is a boundary-condition alternative: it does not provide a future-directed path from A.R.G.O. to the past. The reference science-fiction architecture below instead uses a continuous field route in a chronology-violating five-dimensional bulk. Those are competing ways to alter the causal story, not two parts of one solved engine. The moving-mouth wormhole remains a separate general-relativity comparison, and the two-bubble model remains a test-traveler comparison. None supplies the required five-dimensional source or the reverse-playback map by itself.

A.R.G.O.’s proposed quantum drive: an entangled chronal echo#

Now let the science fiction begin. A.R.G.O. carries two matched quantum resonators whose fields are prepared in a two-mode squeezed state,

\[ |\Psi(r,\phi)\rangle=\frac{1}{\cosh r}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(e^{i\phi}\tanh r)^n|n\rangle_A|n\rangle_B,\qquad \bar n=\sinh^2 r. \]

The parameter \(r\) sets the shared excitation level and \(\phi\) sets the relative phase. In the fictional drive, a controlled interaction \(H_{\rm int}(t)=g(t)\,\hat O_A(t)\hat O_B(t)\) couples the resonators, in spirit like the boundary interaction in the Gao–Jafferis–Wall construction. A.R.G.O.’s compact resonators are not those boundary theories; the interaction is a design postulate that must be given a microscopic Hamiltonian and energy budget. Entanglement helps prepare and read the channel; it is not the channel and cannot send a chosen message by itself. The reference architecture assumes a chronology-violating five-dimensional bulk geometry that supports the field route; it does not assume that entanglement creates a traversable throat. A wormhole-like throat could be one geometric ingredient, but then its stress-energy and clock map must be solved separately. A.R.G.O. and its crew stay home. A low-energy information-bearing field follows the bulk route continuously, and a local neural interface turns the arriving field into a lived remote viewpoint.

There is a real mathematical seed for this device. In a particular theoretical setting, Gao, Jafferis, and Wall showed that coupling two boundaries of an entangled black-hole system can create negative averaged null energy and make an Einstein–Rosen bridge traversable. A.R.G.O. extends that result to compact resonators and a useful signal channel; neither extension follows from the paper. The boundary coupling is essential in the mathematical example—entanglement alone does not open the passage. ER=EPR inspires the geometry; the traversable-wormhole construction inspires the control interaction.

Keep four time maps separate#

A.R.G.O.’s premise asks one proposed field to do four jobs: map the crew clock to a remote viewpoint \(q(\tau_A)\), map a ship-side pulse to a historical emission event \(F(s)\), carry ordinary light from that emission to a witness \(W(X)\), and close any resulting chronology loop with a matched return state. These are distinct boundary conditions on one coupled solution. A constant mouth-clock offset may place a pulse in an earlier era, but the standard equal-clock throat map has unit slope: it does not scan millennia in minutes or reverse the sequence. The ordinary photon path then determines when a witness sees the light; it cannot be counted as the crew’s return signal. A positive timing margin alone does not prove loop closure unless the computed return path intersects the correct world-tube and matches the field state.

The technical appendix now lays out these maps and their missing physics. For the reference mission, a useful result is not merely “the light could appear at an earlier date,” but a single solution that predicts the crew’s view, the historical source’s direction and spectrum, each witness’s received track, the return information, and the closure residual. If those outputs cannot be produced by the same metric and conserved field equations, that parameter set is not a viable A.R.G.O. mission—even inside the story’s physics.

A more ambitious quantum doorway: entangle, open, time-shift#

Here is a bolder design for the story. Do not ask a Bell pair to whisper across the universe. Ask a vast entangled quantum system to help define the shape of the route, then use a controlled interaction to open that route briefly. A.R.G.O. would be a clock, control room, and viewpoint decoder; the moving part of the design would be the geometry and the information-bearing field.

In the most studied theoretical sketch, the two sides begin in a thermofield-double-like state,

\[ |\mathrm{TFD}\rangle=Z^{-1/2}\sum_n e^{-\beta E_n/2}|n\rangle_L|n\rangle_R, \]

where \(L\) and \(R\) label two quantum systems, \(E_n\) are their energy levels, \(\beta=1/(k_B T)\) is inverse temperature, and \(Z\) normalizes the state. The proposed control is an interaction between the sides,

\[ H_{\rm link}(t)=g(t)\sum_a \hat O_L^{(a)}\hat O_R^{(a)}. \]

With dimensionless \(\hat O_L\) and \(\hat O_R\), \(g(t)\) has units of energy; a microscopic model must say what supplies that work and where the control field’s stress-energy goes.

In the specialized holographic model, this coupling changes the quantum stress near the Einstein–Rosen bridge. The important quantity is the averaged null energy along a ray with tangent \(k^\mu\),

\[ \mathcal A=\int d\lambda\,\langle T_{\mu\nu}\rangle k^\mu k^\nu. \]

The Gao–Jafferis–Wall construction makes \(\mathcal A<0\) for its chosen interaction and state; the gravitational response can then let a signal cross a bridge that was previously non-traversable. In semiclassical gravity the backreaction belongs in

\[ G_{\mu\nu}+\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}=\frac{8\pi G}{c^4}\langle T_{\mu\nu}^{\rm total}\rangle_{\rm ren}. \]

This is the exciting science seed: quantum information changes a stress tensor, the stress tensor changes geometry, and the geometry changes which signals can pass. It is not “entanglement is a tunnel.” The bridge in the ER=EPR conjecture is ordinarily non-traversable; the extra interaction is what opens the theoretical example. Gao, Jafferis, and Wall derive this in a particular anti-de Sitter black-hole setting, not for a garden-sized saucer. Their setup does not violate causality. A.R.G.O.’s pastward mouth clock would be another, separate step. A 2022 quantum-processor experiment reproduced selected dynamics of a simplified holographic model with a nine-qubit circuit; it did not create a wormhole in space or send a signal through one (Nature).

For A.R.G.O., make the speculative extrapolation explicit: replace the two little resonators with a large, strongly interacting quantum network whose effective geometry has two mouths. The ship-side mouth sits beside the stationary dome. The target-side mouth is a boundary condition on a field mode associated with a chosen historical Earth event; it is not hardware or an entangled particle that someone secretly installed there. The story’s new law says that the prepared quantum state encodes the geometric connection; a smooth control pulse \(g(t)\) opens a finite window; and a low-energy probe or data field takes a continuous path through the throat. At the target event, a time-varying arriving field modulates \(Z\) through the \(\Psi_\Sigma F^2\) coupling and can pump electromagnetic modes, producing a visible flash without a pre-positioned lamp. The witness sees ordinary future-directed photons. If the crew wants to see the witness’s view, a second field path returns measurements to A.R.G.O. The entangled state can help establish the shared code and timing reference, but each leg still needs a physical channel.

The new ingredient that makes this time travel is a clock offset, not entanglement. Let \(\Delta_{\rm past}\) be the earlier-than-present shift produced by the two mouth histories, \(\delta_{\rm th}\) the finite throat transit contribution, and \(T_{\rm ext}\) the travel time needed for a specified causal return to the entry mouth. The geometry must deliver

\[ \Delta_{\rm eff}=\Delta_{\rm past}-\delta_{\rm th}>T_{\rm ext}, \]

with a margin larger than timing and route uncertainty if the story needs a robust closed loop. This is the loop test derived in Appendix B. A traversable throat alone moves information between mouths; it does not make either mouth historical. A time-shifted throat can give one earlier appearance, but an ordinary equal-clock map still preserves the order of signals. A five-minute scan across 5,000 years needs the extra transfer law \(|dq/d\tau_A|\simeq 5.26\times10^8\) as well as the past offset. No known entanglement protocol supplies that factor.

The bootstrap question: where was the other half of the pair?#

There is a loop hidden inside the word pre-shared. To open an entanglement-assisted channel, its two ends must already share a quantum state. But A.R.G.O. cannot send an ordinary preparation pulse to an endpoint in the historical scene before the mission begins. The past-side partner cannot simply be installed by an earlier crew: that would change this mission into a material time-travel story. So the partner is not a battery or a box of particles waiting in the past. In this fiction, it is a field degree of freedom whose state is defined as part of the complete, time-shifted spacetime solution.

One way to keep that loop from contradicting itself is to demand a fixed point. Let \(\rho_C\) be the state of the chronal field as it re-enters its own past, \(\rho_Q\) the query state prepared aboard A.R.G.O., and \(U_{g,\Delta}\) the joint evolution generated by the gate-opening interaction and the time-shifted geometry. A Deutsch-style consistency condition is

\[ \rho_C=\Phi_{g,\Delta}(\rho_C),\qquad \Phi_{g,\Delta}(\rho)=\operatorname{Tr}_Q\!\left[U_{g,\Delta}(\rho_Q\otimes\rho)U_{g,\Delta}^{\dagger}\right]. \]

This equation is not yet a route calculation. In the navigation model, \(U_{g,\Delta}\) must be derived from local interactions and field evolution along a single continuous path \(\Gamma\) in the solved metric. A free matrix selected to produce a desired answer does not say where or when the interaction happened, how the field got there, or whether the pulse cleared matter and hazards. The outgoing field state must also be propagated to the receiver and, for a sighting, coupled to a powered emitter whose photons are then traced to a witness.

Even a simple qubit loop shows why state consistency is only a first filter. Let the query register be fixed in \(|1\rangle_Q\), let the loop register be \(C\), and take \(U\) to be a controlled-NOT with \(Q\) controlling \(C\). Then \(\Phi(\rho_C)=X\rho_CX\), where \(X\) is the Pauli bit-flip operator. Every state

\[ \rho_C=\tfrac12(I+r_xX),\qquad -1\le r_x\le1, \]

is a fixed point. The loop admits a continuum of self-consistent states; the equation does not select which one describes the historical field or what scene the crew will decode. A selection rule must predict observable probabilities, and the field-and-geometry solver must supply the actual interaction rather than this toy gate.

The state that comes around the loop must be the same state that went in. This is a model choice inspired by quantum theories of closed timelike curves, not a consequence of ordinary quantum mechanics in a laboratory. It makes the mission a boundary-value problem: solve for a self-consistent field state and its continuous history, then ask what view that state lets the crew decode. The finite-dimensional Deutsch map above has at least one mathematical fixed point; the coupled spacetime and field equations may still have no physically admissible solution, and multiple fixed points would require a selection rule and observable probabilities. Other proposals, such as post-selected teleportation, impose different rules and can give different predictions (Deutsch, 1991; Lloyd et al., 2010).

The fixed-point equation does not open a throat, send energy, or guarantee that the field state is stable. Those still require the semiclassical Einstein equation, a conserved stress-energy ledger, and a route with collision clearance. It only gives the quantum loop a consistency test. In the story’s more lyrical language: the echo cannot arrive from a past that has no place for it; the whole loop must find one note that agrees with itself. A.R.G.O.’s clock advances, the hull stays home, and the scene is experienced only if the loop’s field solution carries a decodable record.

There is also an information bill. As a deliberately extravagant benchmark, imagine reconstructing 5,000 years of uncompressed 1920×1080, 24-bit RGB video at 30 frames per source second. That is \(4.73\times10^{12}\) frames, about \(2.94\times10^{19}\) bytes (29.4 exabytes), compressed into five minutes at about \(98\) petabytes per second. The onboard interval between frame addresses is only about 63 picoseconds. Human perception does not require a full video archive; a sparse description of a few lights could be vastly smaller. But quantum correlations do not erase the information requirement: the channel must carry enough distinguishable states to support whichever view the crew is promised.

For scale, ideal superdense coding can encode two classical bits in one transmitted qubit when the receiver already shares a Bell pair with the sender. Even granting that ideal protocol, the raw-video benchmark would require at least \(3.9\times10^{17}\) transmitted qubits per second. The qubit still has to travel through a channel, and the shared pair must be prepared and preserved; pre-shared entanglement alone carries no chosen message (Bennett and Wiesner). A practical story can lower this requirement by sending event markers—“five lights here, moving south, this brightness”—instead of a movie, but it must say what details are discarded and how the returned view is reconstructed.

The doorway has four gauges for the navigator: entanglement fidelity (does the shared state survive?), averaged null energy (does the gate-opening interaction have the required sign and duration?), loop margin (does the time-shifted route close?), and channel capacity (does enough information arrive before the gate shuts?). Low entanglement fidelity raises decoding errors or demands costly error correction; the wrong null-energy pulse leaves the bridge shut; a failed loop test means the chosen causal return does not close; and insufficient capacity yields a blurred or incomplete scene. This chain gives quantum theory a real job in the fiction while preserving the hard boundary: the published holographic results do not supply a compact, stable, Earth-scale time machine.

In the book’s language: entanglement writes the score, the controlled negative-energy pulse lifts the bridge, the clock offset points its far end toward an older scene, and ordinary light makes the scene visible.

The most speculative sentence in the design is also its clearest research question: can a controllable entangled quantum network source enough negative averaged null energy to open a finite throat, then change the throat’s clock map by billions while keeping the field state coherent and the loop self-consistent? The story’s answer can be yes. Its calculation must still solve the geometry, source, quantum channel, and witness image together.

The key speculative step is not a device that flips time like a film reel. It is a spacetime with a continuous chronal echo: the signal always advances along its local future direction, while the chosen Earth-time coordinate decreases along part of its route. In ordinary globally ordered spacetime, no such route exists. In a spacetime containing a closed timelike curve (CTC), a future-directed signal can return to an event in its own past. The crew’s proper time \(\tau_A\) still advances. The remote event label \(q\) can decrease only because the signal path passes through a region where this coordinate is not a global clock.

Separate temporal magnification from time reversal#

Let \(T_A\) be the crew’s mission duration and \(\Delta Q\) the interval of remote history to be sampled, both in the same units. A simple event-selection law is

\[ q(\tau_A)=q_0+sR(\tau_A-\tau_0),\qquad R=\frac{\Delta Q}{T_A}>0,\qquad s\in\{-1,+1\}. \]

The book’s original choice \(s=-1\) asks for more than the mission needs if the goal is to view an interval of the past in order: a later onboard sample corresponds to an earlier remote event. That reverses the order of scene markers and, for a directly transferred wave, reverses the sign of its phase frequency. Choosing \(s=+1\) instead scans from an ancient event toward a later one. By choosing the interval endpoints to remain before the crew’s present, the scenes stay historical while appearing in their ordinary chronological order.

Choose \(q_0\) at an ancient event and let \(q\) advance through a historical interval. The crew can then experience that interval at high speed while the selected events remain in their ordinary chronological order; this is a map between event labels and the crew’s experience, not a claim that people in the remote history feel their clocks speed up. For a 5,000-year interval sampled in five onboard minutes, \(R=5.2596\times10^8\) remote seconds per crew second. One nanosecond of onboard timing uncertainty becomes 0.526 seconds in the reconstructed history; 30 samples per remote second would require about 63.4 picoseconds between onboard samples. If the signal’s phase is directly reparameterized, a 550 nm optical carrier would map to roughly 1.04 MHz (about 289 m wavelength), so a visible appearance still needs a powered optical emitter at the historical event rather than merely relabeling the crew’s carrier.

This is a useful comparison, not a free physical shortcut. Laboratory time lenses can magnify or compress a photon wavepacket’s time coordinate using dispersive propagation and nonlinear optics, and quantum time lenses can reshape temporal modes. They operate on fields already present in the apparatus; they do not let a laboratory select an arbitrary remote spacetime event or send a signal to it (quantum temporal imaging). A moving-mouth wormhole also produces a Lorentz-factor relation between exterior coordinate times, but that is not automatically a pastward scan. Suppose A is stationary and B is the clock-lagging mouth. Equal-clock matching gives \(d\tau_A=d\tau_B\). While B coasts, \(d\tau_B=dq_B/\gamma\), so the stationary-to-moving map has \(dq_B/d\tau_A=\gamma\); with B lagging, that A-to-B crossing exits on B’s future side. The pastward crossing is B-to-A. When indexed by the crew’s proper clock at the lagging mouth, its ideal map to the stationary mouth is \(T_A=s_B+C\), hence \(dT_A/ds_B=1\). A fixed clock lag changes the exit epoch but leaves a five-minute input spanning five minutes at the exit.

For the 5,000-year/five-minute example, the coordinate-time comparator would need \(\gamma=R=5.2596\times10^8\), or \(1-v/c\simeq1/(2\gamma^2)=1.81\times10^{-18}\). During the corresponding 5,000-year exterior interval, a coasting mouth travels almost 5,000 light-years. Its special-relativistic kinetic energy is \(E_k=(\gamma-1)mc^2\simeq4.73\times10^{25}\) joules per kilogram of mouth mass; this omits the wormhole’s own mass, formation energy, and support fields. These numbers characterize the wrong-direction coordinate stretch in the standard clock-lag setup; they do not establish the needed pastward mind-scan. A path that brings the mouth back to the witness region also needs a solved, hazard-checked worldline. To map five minutes of the lagging mouth’s proper time onto 5,000 years of past source proper time, the throat transfer itself needs a varying offset \(h(s_B)\) with \(dh/ds_B\approx R-1\approx5.2596\times10^8-1\). Quantum-field consistency gate at the chronology horizon#

On the initial globally hyperbolic side, choose a quantum state whose two-point function \(W(x,x')\) has the Hadamard short-distance singularity. This is a precise breakdown result for that class of horizons; it is not a theorem that every conceivable chronology-violating geometry is impossible. In Misner space, explicit calculations also find divergent vacuum stress near the chronology horizon for massive and nonconformally coupled scalar fields (Tanaka and Hiscock).

This creates a hard gate for the A.R.G.O. solver. A classical loop with finite transfer gain does not pass the quantum-field gate. A computable propagator benchmark: images before the horizon#

We can calculate a limited Green response without pretending to solve the chronology horizon. Set the helical displacement \(\mathbf D=0\), suppress the transverse coordinates, and use the chronology-respecting side \(T<0\) of the two-dimensional Misner quotient. With

\[ \rho=2L\sqrt{-T},\qquad \chi=-\frac{\psi+\ln(-T)}{2}, \]

the covering metric becomes \(ds^2=-d\rho^2+\rho^2d\chi^2\). The cover coordinates are \(ct=-\rho\cosh\chi\), \(X=\rho\sinh\chi\); the future direction in this wedge points toward decreasing \(\rho\). The quotient identifies \(\chi\sim\chi+\pi\), and \(\rho=0\) is the chronology horizon. This is a compactified Milne region, not the full five-dimensional A.R.G.O. bulk.

For a periodic massless scalar in 1+1 dimensions, the retarded quotient Green function on this globally hyperbolic side is the method-of-images sum

\[ G_{\rm ret}^{Q}(x,x')=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}G_{\rm ret}^{\mathbb M^{1,1}}(X,\Lambda_nX'), \]

where \(\Lambda_n\) is the \(n\)-fold boost image. Take a source at \((\rho',\chi')\) and a later receiver at \((\rho,\chi)\), with \(0<\rho<\rho'\). The image with winding number \(n\) has squared timelike interval

\[ \sigma_n=\rho'^2+\rho^2-2\rho\rho'\cosh(\Delta\chi+n\pi),\qquad \Delta\chi=\chi-\chi'. \]

It contributes inside the receiver’s past light cone exactly when

\[ |\Delta\chi+n\pi|<\ln(\rho'/\rho). \]

At any fixed pair of events strictly before the horizon, only finitely many integers satisfy this bound. The interactive counts those images. For \(\rho'/\rho=1{,}000\) and \(\Delta\chi=0\), five future-directed covering-space geodesics contribute. In this special 1+1D massless model, each interior-cone Green term has the same constant value, so a unit point source gives a Green-function value five times the single-image value at that receiver event. A finite waveform is integrated against each image’s retarded Green function; the 1+1D interior-cone support produces tails, not five sharp arrivals. Neither statement is an energy gain or a drive-power prediction; both depend on this model’s dimension, field, periodic boundary condition, and prescribed quotient.

As \(\rho\to0^+\) while the source remains farther from the horizon, the allowed winding range grows without bound. In the CTC region, the quotient orbit itself is timelike and has infinitely many windings; the naïve retarded image sum is no longer a finite, uniquely selected response. A possible additional boundary rule would multiply the field amplitude by \(g e^{i\phi}\) on each circuit. Its frequency-domain sum would be

\[ G_{\rm loop}(\omega)=G_0\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}g^n e^{in(\omega T+\phi)}=\frac{G_0}{1-ge^{i(\omega T+\phi)}}. \]

This series converges absolutely only for \(|g|<1\). With equal local normalization for one tracked mode, \(|g|^2\) is the energy fraction that mode retains per circuit; the remaining \(1-|g|^2\) must transfer to other modes, matter, or an explicitly modeled loss channel. At \(|g|=0.7\), that is 49% retained and 51% transferred per turn. If \(|g|=1\), a nonresonant phase can make finite partial sums bounded, but the infinite series still has no ordinary limit; resonant frequencies diverge, and a phase cancellation at one frequency does not regularize a finite-band signal. This attenuation/phase rule is an imposed boundary condition, not a consequence of the Misner metric. The delay-loop panel later in the chapter explores it; it cannot cure the independent quantum-field warning that Misner-space vacuum stress diverges at the horizon (Tanaka & Hiscock) or bypass the scope of the Kay–Radzikowski–Wald result for compactly generated Cauchy horizons (paper). These are not universal theorems against every fictional geometry, but they prevent us from assuming a stable finite propagator through this one.

A compact-time echo: an exact winding benchmark#

We can isolate the echo problem in flat space before asking a five-dimensional field to solve it. Write the 1+1D Minkowski coordinates as \((ct,x)\) and identify points by

\[ (ct,x)\sim(ct+\delta,x+L),\qquad \delta=c\Delta. \]

The squared interval of one identification step is \(L^2-\delta^2\). If \(\delta<L\), the step is spacelike and this quotient is an ordinary spatial cylinder in a boosted frame. If \(\delta=L\), it is null. If \(\delta>L\), it is timelike: following the identification once gives a continuous closed timelike curve. The local metric and light speed have not changed; the global identification has.

For a massless scalar in 1+1D, the retarded Green function on the covering Minkowski space has constant support inside the future light cone. A candidate quotient response is the image sum

\[ G^Q_{\rm ret}(z,z')=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}G^{\mathbb M}_{\rm ret}\!\left(ct-ct'+n\delta,\;x-x'+nL\right). \]

Take a source and receiver at the same quotient event and count nonzero windings \(n=1,2,\ldots\). The \(n\)th image lies in the covering space’s retarded support when \(n\delta\ge |nL|\). Thus the chronology-respecting case has no causal winding return, the null threshold places every image on the light-cone boundary, and the timelike case has infinitely many future-causal covering images. The CTC quotient has no global retarded ordering, so this sum is a diagnostic candidate, not a uniquely defined retarded Green function on the quotient. In this special 1+1D massless model, every strictly interior-cone image has the same normalized amplitude. Truncating after \(N\) turns therefore gives \(G_N/G_1=N\) in the constructive, phase-aligned case: the cutoff response grows without limit as \(N\to\infty\). This exact divergence is specific to this toy dimension, field, and image prescription. It is not a theorem that every CTC geometry has an infinite Green function, nor is it evidence that A.R.G.O. can create this topology.

In natural units (\(c=\hbar=1\)), take a five-dimensional bulk \((\mathcal M_5,G_{AB})\) with a four-dimensional brane \(\Sigma\), induced metric \(g_{\mu\nu}\), and bulk scalar \(\Psi_5\). When a past appearance would create a causal loop#

Separate the appearance from the feedback. Let \(S\) be a present control event aboard A.R.G.O., \(p\) the proposed earlier event where a field makes or redirects light, and \(W\) a witness’s photon-reception event. The sighting route is

\[ S\;\dashrightarrow\;p\;\xrightarrow{\rm light}\;W. \]

The dashed arrow is not yet a physical path. The optical arrow is an ordinary future-directed null path. A separate return route, if the model needs one to close a loop, must be written as \(p\xrightarrow{\Gamma_R}S'\) and must carry a specified field, record, or state. The fact that \(p\) and today’s A.R.G.O. event are timelike separated means that future-directed curves between them exist; it does not prove that the particular sighting, its information, or its energy actually follows one. Nor does light reaching a witness automatically return to the ship.

There are two different candidate models here. Geometric time travel: a complete spacetime contains a continuous future-directed causal curve \(\Gamma_C\) from \(S\) to \(p\), even though \(p\) lies in the exterior past of \(S\). If a specified future-directed return curve \(\Gamma_R\) takes the resulting state from \(p\) back to A.R.G.O.’s world-tube at \(S'\), and \(S'\) is \(S\) or an earlier event on that world-tube from which a continuous waiting segment reaches \(S\), the concatenated route is a closed causal curve; a timelike segment makes it a closed timelike curve. Endpoint event, tangent, field state, and return time must match. Advanced-boundary model: \(G_{\rm adv}(p,S)\ne0\) is a global boundary-value relation in an otherwise ordinary metric. It is not a future-directed curve \(S\to p\), so it does not by itself make a geometric CTC or satisfy the no-jump route requirement. It instead postulates that the later boundary condition controls a field at \(p\). To turn that correlation into a signal, the theory must specify the new causal law and its feedback rule.

The closure check should use A.R.G.O.’s proper-time world-tube, not just a date label. Integrate the return curve to find \(S'\). If it intersects A.R.G.O. at an event with proper time \(\tau_A(S')\), define

\[ \epsilon_{\rm loop}=\tau_A(S')-\tau_A(S). \]

An endpoint on A.R.G.O.’s world-tube with \(\epsilon_{\rm loop}<0\) can close through a finite wait from \(S'\) to \(S\); \(\epsilon_{\rm loop}=0\) requires the endpoint state and tangent to match there; \(\epsilon_{\rm loop}>0\) does not return to the control event. This event test is in addition to the loop-margin estimate \(M_{\rm loop}=\Delta_{\rm past}-T_{\rm ext}\) in Appendix B, which screens whether a proposed exterior return can reach the entry world-tube at all. The exact Bethlehem epoch and the physical return carrier remain unknown, so no numerical loop closure can yet be claimed.

This distinction corrects the tempting shorthand \(S\to p\to W\to S\). The final arrow cannot be inferred just from Earth’s ordinary timelike worldline: the model must identify what physical degrees of freedom carry the loop state from the past event to the present control system. The sighting photons, a witness’s durable record, and the advanced scalar are different candidate carriers with different worldlines, energy budgets, and boundary conditions. If no return carrier is specified, report an earlier appearance as an open-channel scenario, not as a solved closed-loop mission.

For a geometric channel, Astrarium must integrate \(\Gamma_C\) and \(\Gamma_R\) in the same metric and verify closure at the event and state level. Any information, light, or matter sent around the loop must obey one declared consistency rule. The model could seek a globally self-consistent history, or introduce a different rule such as branching histories; these are different physical assumptions and predict different outcomes. General-relativistic studies of closed-timelike-curve spacetimes show that ordinary initial-value questions require special care (Friedman et al., 1990). Screen both paths and the optical leg for collisions and field interactions. A solution that only arrives at \(p\) is incomplete: it must also predict the light at \(W\) and, if a causal loop is claimed, return an identified state to \(S\).

The reachability condition can be written without assigning a fictional speed to the signal. Let \(S=z_A(\tau_s)\) be a present-day emission/control event, and let \(p\) be the earlier event where the manifestation field is generated. A candidate channel is a curve \(\gamma^\mu(\lambda)\) with

\[ \gamma(0)=S,\qquad \gamma(1)=p,\qquad g_{\mu\nu}\dot\gamma^\mu\dot\gamma^\nu\leq 0, \]

with \(\dot\gamma^\mu\) future-directed everywhere in the chosen metric. If an exterior time label has \(T(p)<T(S)\), that label decrease must follow from the global causal geometry; it cannot be imposed by hand. In a globally hyperbolic spacetime, such a future-directed path to an earlier event is unavailable. If the selected geometry supplies it, the full curve, any causal return path, and its field source must be solved and checked for closure, stability, and consistency. A plotted segment satisfying only the endpoint equation is not a solution.

This gives the simulator a strict contract: (1) integrate A.R.G.O.’s stationary worldline and onboard clock; (2) integrate the continuous information/field path through the proposed geometry; (3) map that path to the crew’s experienced remote-event sequence; and (4) propagate the visible light to the witness and calculate the predicted observation. 4. Reconstructing the sky#

4.1 Stars#

Gaia astrometry gives reference positions and proper motions for many stars. Astrarium propagates catalogued motion from the reference epoch to the selected year and precesses directions into the equator of date. For stars with measured radial velocity and a usable distance, a linear three-dimensional motion model captures perspective effects. Where radial velocity or distance is absent, the model uses angular proper motion. This is an approximation: constant velocity omits Galactic acceleration, unmeasured radial motion, and close gravitational encounters. Precision should be described as object- and epoch-dependent.

4.2 Planets and the Moon#

Planetary positions are evaluated for the selected date from JPL Horizons vectors based on DE441 where available, with a compact approximate-position fallback for some bodies. Horizons vectors are cached and interpolated between samples. The fallback is useful for continuity, but is not equivalent to a high-precision ephemeris. Planetary-system barycenters and planet centers are not interchangeable, especially when comparing a planet with its moons.

The Moon is handled through time-tagged JPL Horizons vectors and interpolation, with a compact orbit approximation available while data is missing. The lunar position is especially important for the Earth–Moon barycenter split, local sky appearance, and route encounters. Network availability and lunar model coverage must remain visible to the user.

4.3 Natural satellites#

The route and rendering catalogs include 79 named moons across Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune, and Pluto. Their JPL solutions have object-specific time spans; a solution may cover only decades or centuries, not the full 6,000-year clock. JPL’s planetary satellite ephemeris table is therefore queried for its actual kernel ranges. A missing kernel state is unknown, not evidence that the moon was absent or safely distant.

Only selected moon gravitational parameters are currently included in route integration. Other catalogued moons can participate in collision screening when their states and radii are available, but do not necessarily perturb the computed vehicle trajectory. Distant irregular satellites also require sufficiently large screening regions; a narrow parent-centered cutoff can miss a real encounter.

5. Historical traffic: satellites and spacecraft#

“All satellites” spans at least two distinct datasets. Natural moons are celestial bodies with long-term orbital solutions of uneven coverage. Artificial satellites are human-launched objects whose trajectories begin at launch and change through maneuvers, drag, deployments, failures, and fragmentation. No 5,000-year ephemeris can supply the future histories of objects not yet launched or the unobserved histories of objects whose records are missing.

For Earth-orbiting objects, Astrarium can use dated TLE/OMM element sets and SGP4/SDP4 propagation. The integrated ETH Zurich archive begins continuous collection on 2023-03-10 and has object and date gaps. User-imported TLE, 3LE, OMM JSON, or GP CSV files can extend local history. Propagation is limited to 60 days from each element epoch, because old orbital elements rapidly lose validity. The system also includes a curated set of reconstructed interplanetary mission histories from NAIF SPICE and JPL Horizons. These records represent selected missions, not every spacecraft.

The correct data contract for any track is: object identity, source and provenance, time scale, reference frame, time span, sample cadence, uncertainty where known, and propagation method. Gaps and out-of-coverage times must remain explicit. A blank region in a plot must never silently mean empty space.

10. Motion while “staying still”: frame-dependent velocity#

“Staying still” has meaning only relative to a specified reference frame and observer. A person standing on Earth’s surface is approximately at rest in the rotating Earth-fixed frame, but moves relative to an Earth-centered inertial frame, the Solar System barycenter, the Galactic center, and the cosmic microwave background (CMB) frame. There is no experimentally privileged Newtonian absolute rest frame. The CMB frame is a measurable cosmological convention—the frame in which the CMB dipole vanishes—and is useful for quoting a large-scale velocity, not a universal navigation origin.

For a surface observer at geocentric latitude \(\varphi\), the rotational speed relative to Earth’s spin axis is approximately

\[ v_{\rm rot}=\omega_\oplus R_\oplus\cos\varphi, \]

where \(\omega_\oplus\) is Earth’s sidereal angular rotation rate and \(R_\oplus\) is its equatorial radius. At the equator this is about 0.465 km/s (1,670 km/h); at latitude 40° it is about 0.356 km/s. The value depends on latitude, altitude, Earth orientation, and whether the observer is attached to the crust or moving relative to it.

Earth’s mean orbital speed around the Sun is about 29.78 km/s. The Sun’s average orbital speed around the Milky Way is about 230 km/s, with caveats: the Galaxy is not a rigid body and the value depends on the adopted Galactic model and local standard of rest. The Solar System’s velocity inferred from the CMB temperature dipole is about 369 km/s. These are vector quantities in different frames and directions; they are not scalar speeds to add. Successive velocities must be transformed between frames. Special-relativistic velocity composition is

\[ \mathbf u\oplus\mathbf v = \frac{1}{1+\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v/c^2} \left[\mathbf u+\frac{\mathbf v}{\gamma_u}+\frac{\gamma_u}{1+\gamma_u}\frac{\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v}{c^2}\mathbf u\right], \qquad \gamma_u=(1-u^2/c^2)^{-1/2}, \]

when \(\mathbf v\) is measured in the rest frame reached by the first boost. For solar-system trajectory geometry at these speeds, a declared inertial frame and a Galilean vector sum are adequate to excellent approximation; relativistic transforms matter for precision timing, high-speed vehicles, and consistent frame changes. The implementation should compose state transforms at explicit epochs rather than add headline speeds.

The “Phoenix Lights” label also covers reports from different times and observers. Do not combine all of them into one trajectory without event-by-event timestamps and source records. The reference account here is specifically NUFORC Sighting 8345; its date caveat, altitude uncertainty, and single-observer status belong in any data model.

A computable clock-and-light-cone example#

Here is a reproducible timing exercise, not a reconstruction of the reported objects. Set a hypothetical control event to 2026-10-18 03:15 UTC and use the report’s approximate 1997-03-13 8:15 p.m. Phoenix local time as a nominal witness event (1997-03-14 03:15 UTC). Place a notional point-like light-producing event 10 km from the witness; this is a timing proxy, not a claim that a lamp or other device was present. The ordinary photon leg then takes \(R/c=33.36\,\mu\mathrm{s}\), so the field-to-light conversion must occur at about 1997-03-14 03:14:59.999967 UTC. With zero assumed throat transit, the required mouth-clock offset is

\[\Delta_{\rm clock}=t_{\rm control}-t_{\rm witness}+\delta_{\rm throat}+R/c\approx29.5961670\;\mathrm{yr}.\]

To give that offset a continuous, explicit kinematic history, choose the prescribed flat-exterior profile \(\beta(u)=0.8\sin(2\pi u)\), with \(u=t/\mathcal T\). Integrating the moving mouth’s proper time, \(\tau_B=\int_0^{\mathcal T}\sqrt{1-\beta(t)^2}\,dt\), gives \(\mathcal T\approx157.888\) exterior years and \(\tau_B\approx128.292\) years. The mouth reaches about 40.206 light-years from its starting point, travels about 80.412 light-years in total, and has a peak coordinate acceleration near \(0.3024\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\). These are values for a chosen smooth worldline; the model does not place the source in Phoenix or make a hull appear there.

The calculation is available as estimate_sighting_wormhole_route.py; its sampled history is phoenix-conditional-mouth-history.csv. For example, change --range-km, --throat-seconds, or --beta-max to see which assumptions drive the result. The default 10 km range is an illustrative input, not a measured distance. The report time is approximate, and the script holds the exterior endpoints fixed in a flat frame: it does not include Phoenix’s rotating-Earth state, Solar-System motion, Galactic motion, a solved throat metric, field-to-light physics, or collision analysis. It calculates a past emission event plus a candidate mouth clock history, not a material A.R.G.O. trajectory. A complete manifestation model must propagate the chronal field to that event and calculate either field-only conversion or coupling to actual target matter, with its energy-momentum and optical visibility.
